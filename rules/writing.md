# Writing in English

Write the way a careful native speaker writes a good paper or a good README: plain, specific, and in
an order that makes sense to someone who wasn't there.

## Do

- Lead with the point. One idea per sentence; vary sentence length.
- Concrete subjects and active verbs. "The solver halves the step when the residual grows", not "a
  reduction of the step size is performed".
- Numbers with units and the conditions they hold under.
- Define every symbol and term at first use. Use one name per concept throughout.
- In `details/`: "we", present tense for the method, past tense for experiments that were run.
- Support every claim about prior work with a citation you have read.

## Avoid

These read as machine-written. Rewrite the sentence instead of swapping a synonym.

- Inflated words: delve, crucial, pivotal, robust (as filler), seamless, leverage, utilize,
  comprehensive, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, intricate, realm, landscape, tapestry, testament,
  showcase, underscore, foster, harness, unlock, elevate.
- Filler frames: "It is worth noting that", "It is important to", "In order to", "This ensures
  that", "plays a key role", "In today's world", "In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall,".
- Signposting the document: "In this section we will…", "As mentioned above".
- Reflexive triplets and "not only … but also" constructions.
- Chains of "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally".
- Em dashes used for drama. Use a comma, a colon, or a new sentence.
- Rhetorical questions, exclamation marks, emoji, bold scattered through prose.
- Stacked hedges ("may potentially help to"). State the claim at the strength the evidence supports.
- Closing sentences that restate the paragraph.
